## Item 1 — Problem 5.2 (attention at the uniform truth) graded RESOLVED

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

The auditor's reduction is exact, and it covers the full generality of Problem 5.2 — all $(v, T, e)$, both parts (a) and (b) — not a special case. Checked step by step:

1. **Zero-set identification $W_0 = \lbrace EUP = 0\rbrace $, $P = I_v - \tfrac1v \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}$.** Softmax output is uniform iff the centered logit $\ell P$ vanishes (softmax is invariant under adding constants; uniform iff all logits equal). *Forward:* for a constant context $x = (a,\dots,a)$, every attended embedding is $E_a$, and the attention weights sum to 1, so $\ell(x) = E_a U$ regardless of $Q$ and $T$; uniformity on these $v$ contexts forces $E_a U P = 0$ for every $a$, i.e. $EUP = 0$. *Converse:* if $EUP = 0$ then for any context, $\ell(x)P = \sum_t \alpha_t(x)\  E_{x_t} U P = 0$ term by term. Both directions verified; the file's heuristic (line ~373) that attention enters as a degree-5 correction is true as a Taylor statement but irrelevant to the zero-set and germ: centering kills the attention contribution *exactly*, at every parameter, not just to leading order.

2. **Two-sided comparability $K \asymp \Vert EUP\Vert _F^2$ on bounded sets.** $\mathrm{KL}(\text{unif}\ \Vert \ \mathrm{softmax}(\ell))$ as a function of the centered logit is $\log\sum_y e^{\ell_y} - \tfrac1v\sum_y \ell_y + \text{const}$: strictly convex on $\mathbf 1^\perp$, minimum 0 at 0, Hessian $\tfrac1v P > 0$ on $\mathbf 1^\perp$; hence $\asymp \Vert \ell P\Vert ^2$ on any bounded logit set. Lower bound on $K$ from the $v$ constant contexts (a fixed positive fraction of inputs) gives $c\Vert EUP\Vert _F^2$; upper bound from convexity of $\Vert \cdot\Vert ^2$ over the attention weights gives $\Vert \ell(x)P\Vert ^2 \le \max_a \Vert E_a U P\Vert ^2 \le \Vert EUP\Vert _F^2$. Constants uniform on any parameter ball. By the file's own (audited-correct) Lemma 2.4(iii), the local pair $(\lambda, m)$ of $K$ at every point equals that of $\Vert EUP\Vert _F^2$.

3. **Reduction to reduced-rank regression.** Writing $\bar U = UC$ for orthonormal $C \in \R^{v\times(v-1)}$ spanning $\mathbf 1^\perp$: $\Vert EUP\Vert _F^2 = \Vert E\bar U\Vert _F^2$, with $Q$ (all $e^2$ coordinates) and $U\mathbf 1$ ($e$ coordinates) exactly flat; flat coordinates change neither pole location nor order of the zeta integral. The germ is that of the reduced-rank population loss $\Vert BA\Vert _F^2$ with $B = E \in \R^{v\times e}$, $A = \bar U \in \R^{e\times(v-1)}$, i.e. $(M, N, H, r) = (v,\  v{-}1,\  e,\  0)$, as the auditor claims. Hypothesis matching: the RLCT is a germ invariant of $K$, so the mismatch between the attention model's categorical/softmax likelihood and Aoyagi–Watanabe's Gaussian likelihood is immaterial once reduced to germs (this is what step 2 licenses); the uniform truth maps to the zero (rank-0) truth; activation plays no role after the exact centering. No hypothesis of the template is violated.

4. **Local pair at 0, and part (b) for every $R$.** $\Vert E\bar U\Vert _F^2$ is homogeneous, so $\lambda(tw) = \lambda(w)$, $m(tw) = m(w)$ for $t \neq 0$; lower semicontinuity (file, §2.3, audited correct) along $tw \to 0$ gives $\lambda(0) \le \lambda(w)$ for all $w \in W_0$. So the minimum over $W_0 \cap \bar B_R$ is $\lambda(0)$ for every $R > 0$ — part (b), $R$-independent. For the multiplicity at 0: any small ball at 0 contains a ball around a scaled copy $tw^\ast $ of any minimizer, and positivity of the zeta integrand then forces pole order at 0 $\ge$ pole order at $w^\ast $; hence the local pair at 0 is exactly the tabulated global pair. (The auditor asserted these two points without argument; the arguments exist and are short, so the conclusion stands.)

5. **The formula.** Substituting $(v, v{-}1, e, 0)$ into the file's Theorem 3.1: cases 2 and 3 are vacuous ($e \ge 1$); case 1 applies iff $e \le 2v-1$, with parity $M{+}N{+}H{+}r = 2v-1+e$ even iff $e$ odd; case 4 applies iff $e > 2v-1$. This reproduces the auditor's three-line formula exactly, including $m = 1$ ($e$ odd), $m = 2$ ($e$ even), and $\lambda = v(v-1)/2$, $m=1$ for $e > 2v-1$; the two branches agree at $e = 2v-1$ (both give $v(v-1)/2$), a passed consistency check. Hand check of the highlighted smallest case $(v,T,e) = (2,2,1)$: $K \asymp (E_1^2+E_2^2)\ \bar u^2$, a product of separate variables with thresholds $1$ and $1/2$, so $(\lambda, m) = (1/2, 1)$ by Lemma 2.4(ii) — matching the formula. **Independent source check of Theorem 3.1 itself:** Drton–Plummer, *A Bayesian information criterion for singular models* (arXiv:1309.0911, JRSS-B 2017), Example 2.2 and Table 1 tabulate the Aoyagi–Watanabe coefficients for $(N,M) = (5,3)$, all model ranks $i$ and true ranks $j$; every entry ($\tfrac32, \tfrac72, 3, \tfrac92, 6, \tfrac92, \tfrac{11}2, \tfrac{13}2, \tfrac{15}2$), and the lone multiplicity-2 entry at $(i,j) = (3,0)$, agrees with the file's four-case formula. They also confirm the coefficient depends on the truth only through its rank, and that the tabulated pair is the global coefficient of the marginal-likelihood expansion.

So Problem 5.2 — both parts, all parameters, with no $T$ or $R$ dependence — is settled by the file's own cited template. The audit's RESOLVED verdict is correct, and the file's openness claims for it (line 58, Problem 5.2, the §8 entry "I do not know it, and I do not believe anyone does", and the closing-section repetition) are wrong as written.

**Minimal repair:** demote Problem 5.2 from open problem to worked example (state $W_0 = \lbrace EUP=0\rbrace $, $K \asymp \Vert EUP\Vert _F^2$, cite Aoyagi–Watanabe with $(M,N,H,r) = (v, v{-}1, e, 0)$ and the resulting three-case formula), delete §8 item 1 and fix the line-58 blanket openness claim plus the misleading "attention enters at degree 5" heuristic paragraph; the genuinely open attention content survives in Problems 5.4–5.5 and Conjecture 5.3.

## Item 2 — Question 9.1 (finite-input ReLU free-energy expansion) graded RESOLVED

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (with one caveat on the write-up standard, below).

First, the quantifier check requested: Question 9.1 does **not** ask for a closed form of $(\lambda, m)$. It asks a yes/no question about the theory — whether the free-energy expansion (2.4) holds with the pair $(\lambda_{\vol}, m_{\vol})$ defined by the Lion–Rolin volume asymptotic — for the specific finite-input model ($p^2$ inputs, $H$ ReLU units, compact semialgebraic $W$ containing an exact fit in its interior, smooth positive prior). The auditor's cell decomposition, if sound, proves "yes"; that genuinely answers the question as posed, and is not a decidability-in-principle evasion. So the verdict turns entirely on the soundness of the argument. Checked step by step:

1. **Finite polyhedral cell partition.** Each preactivation $u_j'(a) + u_j''(b)$ is a *linear* functional of the parameter $w$, and there are finitely many ($H p^2$) unit–input pairs; the sign vectors partition $\R^d$ into finitely many closed polyhedral cells. On each cell every ReLU equals its argument or 0, so $f_w$ is polynomial (degree 2) and $K$ polynomial (degree 4) on the cell, agreeing with an analytic (polynomial) extension to a neighborhood. Correct.

2. **Per-cell free-energy expansion.** On each closed cell $c$ (intersected with $W$; a general semialgebraic $W$ decomposes into finitely many basic pieces, so each piece is compact and defined by finitely many polynomial — hence analytic — inequalities), the evidence restricted to $c$ is the evidence of an analytic model with smooth positive prior on a compact analytically-defined domain. Watanabe's theorem applies per cell: relatively finite variance holds by the file's own Remark 4.1 argument (Gaussian regression on a bounded set); realizability on $c$ holds for the cells whose (compact) intersection with $W_0$ is nonempty; the remaining cells have $\min_c K > 0$ and are exponentially negligible (uniform LLN over a compact parameter set, finitely many inputs). This gives $F_n^{(c)} = nS_n + \lambda_c \log n - (m_c - 1)\log\log n + O_p(1)$ with $(\lambda_c, m_c)$ the pole data of $\int_c K^z \varphi$, and $nS_n$ identical across cells.

3. **Reassembly.** The total evidence is the sum of finitely many positive per-cell evidences (boundary overlaps have measure zero), so $F_n = \min_c F_n^{(c)} + O_p(1)$, i.e. the expansion holds with $\lambda = \min_c \lambda_c$ and $m = \max\lbrace m_c : \lambda_c = \lambda\rbrace $. Likewise $\vol(\varepsilon) = \sum_c \vol_c(\varepsilon)$ with $\vol_c \sim c_c\  \varepsilon^{\lambda_c} (\log 1/\varepsilon)^{m_c-1}$ (same zeta poles per cell, positive coefficients, no cancellation), so $(\lambda_{\vol}, m_{\vol}) = (\min_c \lambda_c,\ \max\text{-among-ties } m_c)$ — the same pair. Hence the answer to Question 9.1 is yes. Elementary and correct.

4. **The one load-bearing external step.** For cells whose intersection with $W_0$ lies entirely in the cell *boundary* (and the file's own indicator-unit realization does sit on cell boundaries: its preactivations take the value 0 on some inputs), step 2 needs Watanabe's theorem for a compact domain defined by analytic inequalities with the true set allowed to touch the boundary. This is the published setting: Watanabe's fundamental conditions (I)–(II), as summarized e.g. in Drton–Plummer (arXiv:1309.0911, Remark 2.1), require a compact parameter space defined by real-analytic constraints and impose no interior condition on the true set — and standard applications (mixture models with the truth on the boundary of the weight simplex, Yamazaki–Watanabe) already depend on this. The file's own Theorem 2.1 paraphrase ("$W_0$ meeting the interior of $W$") is more restrictive than Watanabe's actual conditions; the restriction belongs to the file, not to the theory. So the step is licensed.

**Caveat.** The affirmative answer is a routine *assembly* of published results, but the assembled statement itself appears nowhere in the literature the auditor or I could cite verbatim (the auditor concedes this: "no searched paper states that specialization"). The file's framing — "a new theorem about the theory" — overstates it; the audit's "resolved" slightly understates the residual writing task. On the substance the audit is right: the question as posed is answered, affirmatively, without new ideas, so it cannot stand as an open problem. The genuinely open ReLU content (continuous inputs, growing width — where the cell count diverges) is exactly what the auditor's own scope note excludes, and is not what Question 9.1 asks.

**Minimal repair:** replace Question 9.1 with the observation that finite inputs make $K$ piecewise polynomial on finitely many polyhedral cells, so (2.4) holds with $(\lambda,m) = (\lambda_{\vol}, m_{\vol})$ by per-cell singular learning theory plus domination — and, if a ReLU question is to survive in §9, repose it for continuous input distributions (or width $\to \infty$), where the finite-cell argument genuinely fails. Independently fix the Lion–Rolin citation: the volume/integration result is Lion–Rolin 1998, not the 1997 preparation-theorem paper (auditor's attribution point, spot-checked as plausible but outside this pass's two-verdict scope).
