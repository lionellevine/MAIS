# Bounded Löb at logarithmic proof budgets

*Open problem MAIS-O14 · posed in [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/) as [Question 4.5](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L375) · Status: open.*

*Tags: cooperative AI · agent foundations · Löbian cooperation · proof-based agents · bounded rationality · logic. Difficulty: ★★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

The parametric bounded Löb theorem, due to Critch [[C19]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04184), extends Löb's theorem to proofs of bounded length. For which proof budgets does it hold? Above a certain growth rate it is a theorem; below, it provably fails; between the two lies an undecided logarithmic window — the scale at which a budget can just afford to write down the numeral of its own parameter.

Fix an efficient proof system $S$, by default $\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$: Peano arithmetic with binary numerals, proof length counted in symbols of the written proof, and an abbreviation rule — a proof line may define a fresh symbol to stand for any fixed string, possibly using earlier abbreviations, so that a long expression used many times is paid for once; the count charges every character, definition lines and abbreviation names included. A budget $f$ is **represented**: a computable function fixed together with a formula defining its graph, and $f(k)$ inside a formula abbreviates the expansion through that graph formula (Critch's convention). For $p$ a formula with at most one free variable, $\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k)$ is the formula with free variable $k$ asserting that the instance $p(\overline{k})$ (with $\overline{k}$ the binary numeral of $k$) has an $S$-proof of at most $f(k)$ symbols. Say **PBL holds for $(S,f)$** if for every such $p$: whenever $S \vdash \forall k\ (\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k) \to p(k))$, there exists $\hat{k}$ with $S \vdash \forall k > \hat{k}\ p(k)$ (agenda, Definition 4.2).

Write $\lambda(k) := |\overline{k}|+1$ for the numeral-length budget and $\lambda_{a/b}(k) := \lfloor a\lambda(k)/b \rfloor$ for its rational scalings. The repaired parametric theorem, proved in Sol's companion note [[S26]](../papers/P2/), gives PBL for every *internally regular* budget, where regularity is measured against an expansion function for $S$ — the price of noticing a proof: a fixed nondecreasing represented $\mathcal{E}$ such that any $\varphi$ with an $S$-proof of at most $m$ symbols has an $S$-proof of $\Box_m\varphi$ of at most $\mathcal{E}(m)$ symbols, and $S$ proves $\Box_m\varphi \to \Box_{\mathcal{E}(m)}\Box_m\varphi$. Internal regularity asks that the graph of $f$ be provably total and single-valued and that for each fixed integer $b \geq 1$, $S$ prove $\mathcal{E}(2\lambda(k)) + b\lambda(k) \leq f(k)$ for all $k$ above some standard threshold. In the other direction, PBL fails for every provably bounded budget (take $p = \bot$; the hypothesis holds vacuously), and in systems without abbreviations for every provably sub-logarithmic budget. Abbreviations compress numerals, so that failure proof breaks in $\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$: the window is genuinely open. The **budget algebra** $\mathcal{B}$ is the term algebra generated by constants, $k$, and $\lambda(k)$ under addition, multiplication, composition, and $x \mapsto 2^x$ — search-free, because adding bounded proof search would recreate the counterexample that forced the repair — with each term carrying the canonical graph read off from its defining equations ($S$ proves totality and monotonicity by induction on the term).

**Question ([MAIS-A1, Question 4.5](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L375)).** For $S = \mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$, does PBL hold for the canonical numeral-length budget $f = \lambda$? For the canonical budgets $\lambda_{a/b}$ with fixed $0 < a < b$? More ambitiously, characterize the $\mathcal{B}$-terms $f$ for which PBL holds. The graph is part of each term, so this is a property of a finite syntactic object rather than of an extensional function with many presentations.

A failure proof must survive numeral compression (for $k = 2^{2^m}$, repeated squaring writes $\overline{k}$ in far fewer than $\log k$ symbols); a success proof must run on a budget too small for internal regularity. Either outcome would locate the exact boundary of the Löbian argument. For the failure proposition, the abbreviation wrinkle, and the repaired theorem, see [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/); the refutation that forced the repair is [MAIS-P2](../papers/P2/).
