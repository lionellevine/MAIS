# A time–sample correspondence for the deep linear network

*Open problem MAIS-O78 · posed in [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/) as [Problem 3.11](../agendas/A7/MAIS-A7.tex#L403) · Status: open.*

*Tags: generalization · singular learning theory · training dynamics · developmental interpretability · statistics · probability. Difficulty: ★★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

Two staircases describe learning in the two-layer linear network $L(A,B) = \tfrac12 \Vert  BA - \Phi \Vert _F^2$ (weights $A \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times M}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times H}$ with hidden width $H$; target $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$ of rank $r \le H$ with distinct singular values $s_1 > \dots > s_r > 0$), and they are graded by different variables. Gradient flow $\tau \dot A = -\partial_A L$, $\tau \dot B = -\partial_B L$ from a small aligned initialization of scale $u_0$ learns the singular modes of $\Phi$ one at a time, mode $k$ switching on at $t_k = \tfrac{\tau}{2 s_k} \log \tfrac{s_k}{u_0}$ (Saxe–McClelland–Ganguli [[SMG14]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120)); the Bayesian posterior, as the sample size $n$ grows, walks down a free-energy ladder whose rungs trade the rank-$k$ loss $L_k = \tfrac12 \sum_{\alpha > k} s_\alpha^2$ against the Aoyagi–Watanabe learning coefficient $\lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}}$ [AW05]. Is there a dictionary between training time and sample size that carries one staircase to the other?

Fix $M = N = H$ and $1 \le r < H$, and let the singular values of the target $\Phi(q)$ vary in a one-parameter family $s_1(q) > \dots > s_r(q) > 0$, $q \to \infty$. Use the **aligned initialization**: writing $\Phi(q) = \sum_\alpha s_\alpha(q)\, u_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$ for the singular value decomposition, set $A(0) = \sqrt{u_0} \sum_\alpha e_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$ and $B(0) = \sqrt{u_0} \sum_\alpha u_\alpha e_\alpha^{\top}$, with $e_\alpha$ the standard basis vectors of $\mathbb{R}^H$; gradient flow preserves this form, and each mode strength (the coefficient of $u_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$ in $BA$) obeys its own scalar logistic equation. Put $p_k = [\  3H + k \text{ odd} \ ]$, $\lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}} = \tfrac18\bigl((H+k)(3H-k) + p_k\bigr)$, and $\Delta\lambda_k = \lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}} - \lambda_{k-1}^{\mathrm{AW}} = \tfrac18\bigl(2(H-k) + 1 + p_k - p_{k-1}\bigr)$. Set $A_k(q) = 2\Delta\lambda_k / s_k(q)^2$, assumed $> e$ and $\to \infty$; the **Bayesian crossover** is the large root of $n / \log n = A_k(q)$, namely $n_k(q) = -A_k(q)\  W_{-1}(-1/A_k(q))$ with $W_{-1}$ the lower real branch of Lambert's function, and the **dynamical schedule** is $t_k(q, u_0) = \tfrac{\tau}{2 s_k(q)} \log \tfrac{s_k(q)}{u_0}$. Call the spectral family **well separated** if $n_1(q) < \dots < n_r(q)$ for all large $q$; this is substantive, since a near-flat spectrum can make the ladder skip rungs. The saddle sets $C_k(q)$ are the critical sets of the loss where only the top $k$ modes are fit; the **most degenerate points** of $C_k(q)$ are those deepest in the singularity order, of minimal local learning coefficient and then maximal multiplicity. On the Bayesian side the same architecture is the reduced rank regression model: a parameter $(A,B)$ predicts $y = BAx + {}$standard Gaussian noise, and the posterior is the prior reweighted by this Gaussian likelihood on $n$ samples. Here $\lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}}$ is the invariant of the rank-$k$ model's own optimal set, not the coefficient of the saddle set $C_k$ itself, where the loss is not locally minimized and the coefficient must be defined from the two-sided volume of $\lbrace  |L - L_k| < \varepsilon \rbrace $; whether the two agree is part of [MAIS-O77](MAIS-O77.md). Assigning $C_k$ the free energy $n L_k + \lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}} \log n$ is itself not routine: already at a Morse saddle, the free energy of a window of fixed radius is set by the loss on the window's rim in the escape direction, an order-$n$ correction to $n L_k$ that swamps any $\log n$ term; [MAIS-O81](MAIS-O81.md) asks whether some shrinking window schedule restores the ladder, and its rim computation is what keeps the refutation branch of part (a) live.

**Problem ([MAIS-A7, Problem 3.11](../agendas/A7/MAIS-A7.tex#L403); time versus sample size).** Fix $H, r, \tau$ and a well-separated spectral family as above.

- **(a)** Prove or refute that the Bayesian schedule is realized by the posterior in this joint limit. For each $q$, take i.i.d. data $(x_i, y_i)$ with standard Gaussian $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^H$ and $y_i = \Phi(q) x_i + {}$standard Gaussian noise, and a smooth positive prior on one compact parameter domain $W \subset \mathbb{R}^{2H^2}$ meeting every $C_k(q)$ and its most degenerate points. Write $\psi_{q,n}(k) = n L_k(q) + \lambda_k^{\mathrm{AW}} \log n$ and $k^\ast(q,n) = \arg\min_{0 \le k \le r} \psi_{q,n}(k)$. Show that there are radii $\delta_{q,n} \to 0$ along the joint limit and a constant $c > 0$ such that the following holds. For every sequence $q \to \infty$ and integers $n = n(q) \to \infty$ with $n(q) = O(n_r(q))$ and $\min_{1 \le k \le r} |n(q) - n_k(q)| \ge c \sqrt{n(q) \log n(q)}$, the window $\lbrace  w \in W : \mathrm{dist}(w, C_{k^\ast(q,n)}(q)) < \delta_{q,n} \rbrace $ has maximal posterior mass among the $r+1$ saddle windows, with probability tending to $1$. The exclusion width separates the $\log n$ ladder terms from the $O_p(\sqrt{n})$ empirical fit fluctuations. This problem also requires the saddle-window free energy of [MAIS-O81](MAIS-O81.md); the rim computation there keeps the refutation branch live.
- **(b)** Call a reparametrization **admissible** if $n(t) = \phi\bigl( t / \log(1/u_0) \bigr)$ for one continuous increasing $\phi\colon (0,\infty) \to (0,\infty)$ depending only on $H, r, \tau$, not on $q$, the spectrum, or $u_0$. Take any $u_0 = u_0(q) \to 0$ with $\log(1/s_r(q)) = o(\log(1/u_0(q)))$. Determine which of the following holds uniformly over $1 \le k \le r$ and over all well-separated spectral families: (i) some admissible $\phi$ gives $\phi(t_k / \log(1/u_0)) = n_k (1 + o(1))$; (ii) no admissible $\phi$ gives relative error $o(1)$, but one gives two-sided constant-factor bounds whose constants depend only on $H, r, \tau$; or (iii) even such uniform constant-factor bounds are impossible. Quantify the obstruction. The agenda's heuristic suggests (ii), with $\phi(x) \asymp x^2 \log x$ and the exact mode factor $\Delta\lambda_k$ as the obstruction to (i).

The heuristic, offered as such: eliminating $s_k$ between the two schedules gives $t \asymp \tfrac{\tau \log(1/u_0)}{2\sqrt{2 \Delta\lambda}} \sqrt{n / \log n}$. Training time exchanges against sample size at the rate $t \asymp \sqrt{n / \log n}$ — not $t \asymp n$, as a naive one-step-one-sample account would guess — and any of the three answers in (b) would be informative. See [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/), Section 3.5.
