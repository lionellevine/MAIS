# Does geometric simplicity force a legible mechanism?

*Open problem MAIS-O6 · headline problem 6 of the survey [MAIS-P1](../papers/P1/MAIS-P1.pdf) · canonically formalized in [MAIS-A6](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.pdf) as [Conjecture 4.9](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.tex#L352) · Status: open.*

*Safety: interpretability, generalization — singular learning theory · mechanistic interpretability · developmental interpretability · grokking · simplicity bias. Mathematics: algebraic geometry · harmonic analysis. Difficulty: ★★★ hard.*

A small network trained to add residues mod $p$, when reverse-engineered, turns out to be running a discrete Fourier algorithm: each hidden neuron carries a single frequency. Is that mechanism an accident of training, or is it forced by the geometry of the loss? Singular learning theory says Bayesian learning settles on the exact fits of smallest local learning coefficient, so the question becomes a theorem-or-counterexample: whether the most singular exact fits are the Fourier ones.

Fix an odd $p \ge 3$ and a width $H$. On input $(a,b) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^2$ the network computes $f_w(a,b) = \sum_{j=1}^H V_j\,(u_j'(a) + u_j''(b))^2 \in \mathbb{R}^p$, with lookup tables $u_j', u_j''\colon \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R}$ and output columns $V_j \in \mathbb{R}^p$; the target is the one-hot vector $\delta_{a+b}$. The population loss $K$ is half the mean squared error over the $p^2$ inputs, an explicit degree-six polynomial in the $3pH$ parameters, and $W_0(p,H)$ is its zero set, the variety of exact fits. The **local learning coefficient** $\lambda(w)$ at an exact fit $w$ is the volume-growth exponent: the volume of $\{K \le \varepsilon\}$ inside a small ball around $w$ scales as $\varepsilon^{\lambda(w)}$ up to a power of $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Smaller $\lambda$ means a more degenerate fit, better generalization, and more posterior mass.

Fourier inversion on $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ produces an exact fit $w^F$ at width $2p-1$ whose every unit uses one frequency; set $R_p = 1 + \|w^F\|$ and $\lambda_{\min}(p,H;R_p) = \min\{\lambda(w) : w \in W_0(p,H),\ \|w\| \le R_p\}$. For $0 \le k \le (p-1)/2$ let $\mathcal F_k \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ be the span of $(\cos 2\pi k a/p)_a$ and $(\sin 2\pi k a/p)_a$. A unit $j$ is **active** if $(a,b) \mapsto V_j(u_j'(a)+u_j''(b))^2$ is not identically zero, and **single-frequency** if there is one $k$ with $u_j', u_j'' \in \mathcal F_0 + \mathcal F_k$.

**Conjecture ([MAIS-A6, Conjecture 4.9](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.tex#L352)).** For every odd $p \ge 3$ and $H \ge 2p-1$: every $w \in W_0(p,H)$ with $\|w\| \le R_p$ and $\lambda(w) = \lambda_{\min}(p,H;R_p)$ has all of its active units single-frequency.

In words: within the fixed ball, every maximally degenerate exact fit is built from single-frequency neurons, apart from neurons contributing nothing. A proof would show that geometric simplicity selects an algorithm readable from the weights; a counterexample would split the two notions of simplicity. The structure has been forced once already, under a different selection principle: every maximum-margin solution of this task has single-frequency units (Morwani et al.). The first non-vacuous case is $p = 5$, $H = 9$ (for $p=3$ the condition is vacuous since $\mathcal F_0 + \mathcal F_1 = \mathbb{R}^3$): classify its most singular fits by symmetry, or find a non-Fourier one by computer algebra. Construction of $w^F$, symmetries, and known bounds are in [MAIS-A6](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.pdf).
