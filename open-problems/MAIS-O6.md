# Does geometric simplicity force a legible mechanism?

*Open problem MAIS-O6 · headline problem 6 of the survey [MAIS-P1](../papers/P1/) · canonically formalized in [MAIS-A6](../agendas/A6/) as [Conjecture 4.9](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.tex#L353) · Status: open.*

*Tags: interpretability · generalization · singular learning theory · mechanistic interpretability · developmental interpretability · grokking · simplicity bias · algebraic geometry · harmonic analysis. Difficulty: ★★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

A small network trained to add residues mod $p$, when reverse-engineered by Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith, and Steinhardt [[NCLSS23]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217), turns out to be running a discrete Fourier algorithm: each hidden neuron carries a single frequency. Is that mechanism an accident of training, or is it forced by the geometry of the loss? Watanabe's singular learning theory [W09] says Bayesian learning settles on the exact fits of smallest local learning coefficient, so the question becomes a theorem-or-counterexample: whether the most singular exact fits are the Fourier ones.

Fix an odd $p \ge 3$ and a width $H$. On input $(a,b) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^2$ the network computes $f_w(a,b) = \sum_{j=1}^H V_j\ (u_j'(a) + u_j''(b))^2 \in \mathbb{R}^p$, with lookup tables $u_j', u_j''\colon \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R}$ and output columns $V_j \in \mathbb{R}^p$; the target is the one-hot vector $\delta_{a+b}$. The population loss $K$ is half the mean squared error over the $p^2$ inputs, an explicit degree-six polynomial in the $3pH$ parameters, and $W_0(p,H)$ is its zero set, the variety of exact fits. The **local learning coefficient** $\lambda(w)$ at an exact fit $w$ is the volume-growth exponent: the volume of $\lbrace K \le \varepsilon\rbrace $ inside a small ball around $w$ scales as $\varepsilon^{\lambda(w)}$ up to a power of $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Smaller $\lambda$ means a more degenerate fit, better generalization, and more posterior mass.

Fourier inversion on $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ produces an exact fit $w^F$ at width $2p-1$ whose every unit uses one frequency. Explicitly, write $\omega = 2\pi/p$ and index the output coordinates by $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. One unit is constant, with $u_0' \equiv u_0'' \equiv \tfrac12$ and output column $\bigl(\tfrac1p - \tfrac2p \sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2} (\cos \omega k c + \sin \omega k c)\bigr)_c$; each frequency $1 \le k \le (p-1)/2$ gets four units, with lookup tables $(u'(a),\ u''(b))$ equal to $(\cos \omega k a, \cos \omega k b)$, $(\sin \omega k a, -\sin \omega k b)$, $(\sin \omega k a, \cos \omega k b)$, $(\cos \omega k a, \sin \omega k b)$, the first two with output column $(\cos(\omega k c)/p)_c$ and the last two with $(\sin(\omega k c)/p)_c$. Squaring converts sums of cosines into cosines of sums, and these columns then assemble the discrete Fourier expansion of $\delta_{a+b}$. Set $R_p = 1 + \Vert w^F\Vert $ and $\lambda_{\min}(p,H;R_p) = \min\lbrace \lambda(w) : w \in W_0(p,H),\ \Vert w\Vert  \le R_p\rbrace $, where $\Vert \cdot\Vert $ denotes throughout the Euclidean norm of the full parameter vector. For $0 \le k \le (p-1)/2$ let $\mathcal F_k \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ be the span of $(\cos 2\pi k a/p)_a$ and $(\sin 2\pi k a/p)_a$. A unit $j$ is **active** if $(a,b) \mapsto V_j(u_j'(a)+u_j''(b))^2$ is not identically zero, and **single-frequency** if there is one $k$ with $u_j', u_j'' \in \mathcal F_0 + \mathcal F_k$.

**Conjecture ([MAIS-A6, Conjecture 4.9](../agendas/A6/MAIS-A6.tex#L353)).** For every odd $p \ge 3$ and $H \ge 2p-1$: every $w \in W_0(p,H)$ with $\Vert w\Vert  \le R_p$ and $\lambda(w) = \lambda_{\min}(p,H;R_p)$ has all of its active units single-frequency.

In words: within the fixed ball, every maximally degenerate exact fit is built from single-frequency neurons, apart from neurons contributing nothing. A proof would show that geometric simplicity selects an algorithm readable from the weights; a counterexample would split the two notions of simplicity. The structure has been forced once already, under a different selection principle: every maximum-margin solution of this task has single-frequency units, a theorem of Morwani, Edelman, Oncescu, Zhao, and Kakade [[MEOZK24]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07568). The first non-vacuous case is $p = 5$, $H = 9$ (for $p=3$ the condition is vacuous since $\mathcal F_0 + \mathcal F_1 = \mathbb{R}^3$): classify its most singular fits by symmetry, or find a non-Fourier one by computer algebra. Symmetries of $w^F$ and known bounds are in [MAIS-A6](../agendas/A6/).
