# Admissible budgets for a bounded Löb axiom

*Open problem MAIS-O18 · posed in [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/) as [Problem 5.1](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L448) · Status: open.*

*Tags: cooperative AI · agent foundations · Löbian cooperation · proof-based agents · bounded rationality · logic. Difficulty: ★★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

Löb's theorem has two strengths. The *rule* — from $\vdash \Box P \to P$ infer $\vdash P$ — is what the parametric bounded Löb theorem (recalled below) quantifies. The *axiom* is its internalization, $\Box(\Box P \to P) \to \Box P$, one box deeper; it is what a bounded provability logic would need as its Löb axiom. Which proof budgets make the bounded axiom hold?

Fix an **efficient** proof system $S$: following Critch, a consistent recursively axiomatized extension of Peano arithmetic in which every computable function is representable by a graph predicate, numerals are binary (the numeral $\overline{k}$ of $k$ has length $O(\log k)$), and proofs may introduce abbreviations, so that a long expression used many times is paid for once. Proof length counts symbols. The default $\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$ is a concrete such calculus, fixed in full in the agenda. For $p \in \mathrm{Lang}_1(S)$ (at most one free variable) and a budget $a$, the formula $\Box_{a(k)}\ p(k)$ asserts that the instance $p(\overline{k})$ has an $S$-proof of at most $a(k)$ symbols.

The budgets range over a fixed algebra: $\mathcal{B}$ is the search-free term algebra generated by constants, $k$, and the numeral-length budget $\lambda(k) := |\overline{k}|+1$, closed under addition, multiplication, composition, and $x \mapsto 2^x$. Each term carries the canonical graph read off from its defining equations, so $S$ proves its totality and monotonicity by induction on the term. More generally, a represented budget is a computable function with a designated graph formula; it is internally certified when $S$ proves that graph total and single-valued, together with the monotonicity and eventual-comparison facts its use consumes (for $\mathcal{B}$-terms, the comparison certificates are what [MAIS-O13](MAIS-O13.md) would generate from syntax). Here $u \leq_S^{\ast } v$ means $S$ proves $u(k) \le v(k)$ for all $k$ beyond some standard threshold. The lower bound on $b$ in the statement removes a cheap obstruction: with $p = \bot$ and $a = c = 0$, taking $b$ the length of a fixed proof of $\Box_0 \bot \to \bot$ makes the antecedent provable and the consequent refutable.

**Problem ([MAIS-A1, Problem 5.1](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L448)).** Fix an efficient $S$. Determine for which triples of $\mathcal{B}$-terms $(a, b, c)$, and more generally which triples of internally certified represented budgets, it holds that for every $p \in \mathrm{Lang}_1(S)$ there is $\hat{k}$ with

$$S \vdash \forall k > \hat{k}\ \Bigl(\Box_{b(k)}\bigl(\Box_{a(k)}\ p(k) \to p(k)\bigr) \ \to\  \Box_{c(k)}\ p(k)\Bigr).$$

Restrict first to $\mathcal{B}$-terms satisfying $\lambda \leq_S^{\ast } b$, so the outer box is not a fixed finite search. Prove that some triple of polynomials works for $\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$, and for given $(a, b)$ characterize the admissible $c$. (The admissible $c$ need not have a least element pointwise; for polynomial $c$, determine the least admissible degree.)

In words: whenever the reflection instance at budget $a$ has a proof within budget $b$, the sentence itself must have a proof within budget $c$, and $S$ proves this uniformly for all large $k$. The parametric bounded Löb theorem is the rule form: if $S$ proves $\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k) \to p(k)$ uniformly for all large $k$, and $S$ certifies that $f$ eventually outgrows the cost of noticing proofs of numeral-length scale — of proving, inside $S$, that such a proof exists (the agenda's *internal regularity*) — then $S$ proves $p(k)$ uniformly for all large $k$. The present problem is the statement that theorem would become if its implication moved inside the box. It is also the crux of bounded provability logic: Critch conjectured that every law of Gödel–Löb logic $\mathsf{GL}$ survives when each box receives a polynomial budget ([MAIS-O19](MAIS-O19.md)), and the heuristic proof plan — induction on a $\mathsf{GL}$-derivation, with Critch's bookkeeping lemmas covering the other axioms and rules — leaves the Löb axiom case as the one carrying the real content. For the conventions and that reduction, see [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/).
