# Query complexity of causal model extraction from optimal policies

*Open problem MAIS-O25 · posed in [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/) as [Problem 4.3](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L280) · Status: open.*

*Tags: interpretability · world-model extraction · eliciting latent knowledge · black-box evaluation · complexity theory · statistics. Difficulty: ★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

Each question to an optimal agent — what do you do in this tampered environment? — returns a single bit. Mixing two interventions with weight $q$ and bisecting on the weight at which the agent's action switches converts those bits into binary digits of an interventional probability, and sweeping over interventions recovers the agent's whole causal model. That is the extraction algorithm of Richens and Everitt, run with unlimited exact queries. The accounting question comes first in any finite theory: how many queries?

The framework (a finite causal influence diagram with binary variables and known utility): a skeleton $\mathsf{s}=(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{Z},u)$ fixes $m$ binary chance variables $\mathbf{C}$, an observation set $\mathbf{O}\subseteq\mathbf{C}$, utility parents $\mathbf{Z}\subseteq\mathbf{C}$, and a known utility $u(d,z)$; a single binary decision $D$ with parents $\mathbf{O}$ completes the diagram, so a policy assigns to each observation the probability of playing $D=1$. A model $M=(G,\theta)$ is a directed acyclic graph $G$ on $\mathbf{C}$ with conditional probability tables $\theta$, $K(G)=\sum_i 2^{|\mathrm{Pa}(C_i)|}$ free entries in all. The margin class $\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ requires: every table entry in $[\lambda,1-\lambda]$; the utility gap $g(z)=u(1,z)-u(0,z)$ of absolute value at least $\lambda$, taking both signs among the $z$ agreeing with any given observation on $\mathbf{O}\cap\mathbf{Z}$, and moving by at least $\lambda$ under some flip of each utility parent; every edge of $G$ shifting some entry of its head's table by at least $\lambda$ when its tail flips; and every chance variable in $\mathbf{O}\cup\mathbf{Z}$ or an ancestor of it, at least one unobserved.

The analyst adaptively issues queries $(\sigma,\mathbf{O}',w)$: a mixture $\sigma$, with rational weights, of intervention profiles — a profile applies to the value of each chance variable one of four maps: leave it, fix it to $0$, fix it to $1$, or negate it — an observation mask $\mathbf{O}'\subseteq\mathbf{O}$ withholding the other inputs from the agent, and an observation $w$, a value assignment to $\mathbf{O}'$. Before the interaction an adversary commits, for each task $(\sigma,\mathbf{O}')$, a policy of regret at most $\delta$ — its shortfall in expected utility from the best policy for that intervened, masked task; this problem takes $\delta=0$, exact optimality, randomizing only at exact ties. The **policy-probability oracle** answers each query with the exact probability that the committed policy plays $D=1$ on observing $w$; at $\delta=0$, away from ties, a bit. After $N$ queries the analyst outputs a guess $(\widehat G,\widehat\theta)$; its error is $1$ if the graph is wrong, else the largest entrywise difference of the tables, and the minimax risk at budget $N$ is the smallest worst-case expected error over models in the class and admissible adversaries. Packing plus Fano gives an information-theoretic floor $N=\Omega(K(G)\log(1/\varepsilon))$ (the agenda's Remark 3.3).

**Problem ([MAIS-A2, Problem 4.3](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L280)).** Let $\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ be a compact semialgebraic class satisfying conclusions (a)–(b) of the agenda's Problem 4.2 with modulus $\omega(\delta)=L\delta$. Assume also a richness condition: for some graph $G$ and $\rho>0$, its table-parameter projection contains a $K(G)$-dimensional box of side $\rho$. Determine, up to constants depending on $(m,K,\lambda,L,\rho)$, the minimal budget $N(\varepsilon)$ such that the minimax risk over $\mathcal{N}$ at budget $N(\varepsilon)$, with $\delta=0$ and the policy-probability oracle, is at most $\varepsilon$. In particular: decide whether $N(\varepsilon)\le \mathrm{poly}(K,1/\lambda,L,1/\rho)\log(1/\varepsilon)$, and whether adaptive queries outperform non-adaptive ones by more than a constant factor.

The hypotheses imported from Problem 4.2 ([MAIS-O24](MAIS-O24.md)) say, concretely: (a) any two models in the class sharing an optimal-policy family for every mixture and mask are equal; (b) any two admitting a common policy family of regret at most $\delta$ for both, on every task, differ by at most $L\delta$ in the error above (for all sufficiently small $\delta$) — behavior determines the model, with recovery Lipschitz in the regret. The upper-bound side looks like bookkeeping (bisect each of the $O(K)$ needed critical weights to precision $\varepsilon\cdot\mathrm{poly}(\lambda)$), but it is not: the known reconstruction solves for table entries recursively outward from $\mathbf{Z}$, and errors compound through quotients whose denominators are the quantities the margins bound. Whether the compounding is polynomial or exponential in the depth of the diagram is the real question, and a negative answer would be just as interesting. For the margin conditions (M1)–(M6) in full and the query formalism, see [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/).
