# Opposing staircases

*Open problem MAIS-O7 · headline problem 7 of the survey [MAIS-P1](../papers/P1/) · canonically formalized in [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/) as [Conjecture 3.10](../agendas/A7/MAIS-A7.tex#L367) · Status: open.*

*Tags: generalization · developmental interpretability · singular learning theory · training dynamics · algebraic geometry. Difficulty: ★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

Gradient flow on a deep linear network descends a staircase: it lingers at a saddle where only the $k$ strongest modes of the target are fitted, then drops to the next. Watanabe's free-energy asymptotics assign a complexity invariant to each stage — the local learning coefficient $\lambda$, which controls generalization on the Bayesian side. The conjecture: as the loss falls down its staircase, $\lambda$ climbs an opposing one, step for step. Proved, it would let singular geometry predict the stages of training in advance — the central hope of developmental interpretability.

The setting: a two-layer linear network $x\mapsto BAx$, with $A\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times M}$, $B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times H}$, and hidden width $H>r$, trained by gradient flow on the square loss $L(A,B)=\tfrac12\lVert BA-\Phi\rVert_F^2$ (for whitened inputs, the population loss) against a target $\Phi=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}s_\alpha u_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$ of rank $r$ with distinct singular values $s_1>\dots>s_r>0$. From the small aligned initialization of Saxe, McClelland, and Ganguli ($A(0)=\sqrt{u_0}\sum_\alpha e_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$ and $B(0)=\sqrt{u_0}\sum_\alpha u_\alpha e_\alpha^{\top}$ in the standard basis $e_\alpha$ of $\mathbb{R}^H$, with scale $u_0\to 0$), the mode strengths decouple and grow logistically, and the flow visits the chain of critical sets $C_0, C_1,\dots,C_r$ in order (all saddles except the last: $C_r$ is the optimal set). Here $C_k$ is the set of critical points with $BA=\sum_{\alpha\le k}s_\alpha u_\alpha v_\alpha^{\top}$, the rank-$k$ truncation of the target, and $Av_\alpha=0$, $B^{\top}u_\alpha=0$ for each discarded mode $\alpha$ with $k<\alpha\le r$; the losses along the chain fall, $L_k=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>k}s_\alpha^2$, so $L_0>L_1>\dots>L_r$. At a saddle the usual learning coefficient is undefined (a saddle has descent directions), so the agenda uses the **two-sided** exponent: the Lebesgue volume of nearby parameters whose loss lies within $\varepsilon$ of the saddle's, above or below, shrinks like $\varepsilon^{\lambda(w)}(\log\tfrac1\varepsilon)^{m(w)-1}$. Precisely, the local zeta function $z\mapsto\int_{B_\delta(w)}\lvert L(w')-L(w)\rvert^{z}\,dw'$ extends meromorphically to the complex plane by resolution of singularities; $\lambda(w)$ is minus its largest pole and the multiplicity $m(w)$ is the order of that pole, both independent of the small radius $\delta$. Let $\lambda_k=\inf_{w\in C_k}\lambda(w)$.

**Conjecture ([MAIS-A7, Conjecture 3.10](../agendas/A7/MAIS-A7.tex#L367)).** In this setting, $\lambda_0<\lambda_1<\dots<\lambda_r$ while $L_0>L_1>\dots>L_r$: along the saddle chain that gradient flow visits, the loss falls and the learning coefficient rises, step for step. ($C_k$ is noncompact, so attainment of the infimum is part of the claim.)

In transformer experiments the estimated local learning coefficient climbs as the loss falls through most training stages — most, not all — and on the Bayesian side, the exact free-energy asymptotics of Aoyagi and Watanabe for the deep linear model give the nonnegative increments the conjecture demands, computed at the rank-$k$ models' own optimal sets (whether those coefficients equal the two-sided saddle values is itself part of the problem). The deep linear network is the one model where both staircases are explicit, so it is where the correspondence should first be proved or refuted. For the two-sided definition and the time–sample dictionary the conjecture would calibrate, see [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/).
