# How agent regret limits causal model identifiability

*Open problem MAIS-O27 · posed in [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/) as [Problem 4.6](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L300) · Status: open.*

*Tags: interpretability · world-model discovery · eliciting latent knowledge · black-box evaluation · bounded rationality · statistics · probability. Difficulty: ★★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

An analyst reading a causal model out of an agent's behavior faces two costs of different kinds: sampling error, which more queries repair, and the agent's own imperfection, which no budget repairs. A policy family of regret $\delta$ can be shared by distinct models, and the analyst can never tell those models apart. This problem asks for the exact size of that irreducible ambiguity.

The setting is a binary causal influence diagram. A skeleton $\mathsf{s}=(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{Z},u)$ is a finite set $\mathbf{C}$ of binary chance variables, an observation set $\mathbf{O}\subseteq\mathbf{C}$, a utility-parent set $\mathbf{Z}\subseteq\mathbf{C}$, and a known utility function $u(d,z)\in[0,1]$ of a binary decision $d$ and the utility-parent values $z$: nature runs a causal Bayesian network over $\mathbf{C}$, the agent sees the observed variables, chooses $d$, and is scored. A model $M=(G,\theta)$ is a directed acyclic graph on $\mathbf{C}$ plus conditional probability tables. For $\lambda\in(0,\tfrac12)$, the margin class $\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ consists of the models with no degeneracy finer than $\lambda$: writing $g(z)=u(1,z)-u(0,z)$ for the utility gap, all table entries lie in $[\lambda,1-\lambda]$ (M1); the gap satisfies $|g|\ge\lambda$ (M2) and takes both signs on every slice of the utility-parent values compatible with an observation (M3), so the decision always matters and is never settled by what the agent sees; every edge of $G$ has strength at least $\lambda$ (M4, the strength defined in part (c) below); every chance variable is an ancestor of the utility, at least one of them unobserved (M5); and some flip of a single utility parent moves $g$ by at least $\lambda$, for each parent (M6).

A local intervention applies one of the four maps of $\lbrace0,1\rbrace$ to itself — identity, negation, or a constant ($\mathrm{do}(V\!=\!0)$ or $\mathrm{do}(V\!=\!1)$) — to the value of a single variable $V$, leaving every other mechanism unchanged; a profile assigns one such map to each chance variable, and a mixture $\sigma$ draws a profile from a fixed probability vector. An observation mask restricts the agent's view to a subset $\mathbf{O}'\subseteq\mathbf{O}$, changing what it sees but not the environment. A policy for the shifted task $(\sigma,\mathbf{O}')$ maps each value of the visible variables to a probability of choosing $d=1$; its regret is the shortfall of its expected utility below the optimum for that task, both computed in $M$ under $\sigma$ with only $\mathbf{O}'$ visible. A policy family assigns a policy to every shifted task, and is admissible at level $\delta$ if each has regret at most $\delta$ for its task. The **identified set** $I_\delta(M)$ consists of the models sharing some admissible family with $M$; the error $e(M;M')$ is $1$ if the graphs differ and otherwise the maximum table difference; and $\varphi(\delta;\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ is the worst-case radius $\sup_M \sup_{M'\in I_\delta(M)} e(M;M')$ — the reconstruction error that survives unlimited queries.

**Problem ([MAIS-A2, Problem 4.6](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L300)).** Determine the asymptotics of $\varphi(\delta;\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ as $\delta\to0$: (a) decide whether $\varphi(0^+)=0$ (this refines the agenda's Question 4.1); (b) assuming it is zero, determine $\lim_{\delta\to0}\varphi(\delta)/\delta$ as an explicit function of $(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$, together with the matching statement that some pair of models at distance $c\delta$ is $\delta$-indistinguishable; (c) determine the graph threshold: defining the *strength* of an edge of $M$ as the maximum, over pairs of parent configurations differing only in the tail variable, of the induced table difference (the quantity (M4) bounds below), decide for which pairs $(s,\delta)$ every $M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ carrying an edge of strength at least $s$ has that edge present in every model of $I_\delta(M)$ — and exhibit, for the complementary pairs, an $M$ and an $M'\in I_\delta(M)$ omitting the edge.

Part (c) makes precise which parts of the graph are identifiable: at regret $\delta$, strong edges survive and weak edges blur into the error floor, and the problem asks for the exact exchange rate between edge strength and regret. The known upper half is linear in $\delta$: by the Richens–Everitt theorem ([[RE24]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877), Theorems 1–2), for almost every model a policy family that is $\delta$-optimal on every shifted task determines the tables of the utility's chance ancestors to within $O(\delta)$ entrywise (the constant depending on the model), and recovers a subgraph of the true graph. No lower-bound construction appears in the literature. For the full formalism and the finite-sample problems this floor bounds, see [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/).
