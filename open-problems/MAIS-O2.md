# Behavioral tomography of world-models

*Open problem MAIS-O2 · headline problem 2 of the survey [MAIS-P1](../papers/P1/) · canonically formalized in [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/) as [Problem 4.5](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L290) · Status: open.*

*Tags: interpretability · world-model extraction · eliciting latent knowledge · black-box evaluation · statistics · probability · complexity theory. Difficulty: ★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

Tomography reconstructs the inside of a body from measurements taken outside it. Here the body is an agent, the measurements are its actions, and the object to reconstruct is the causal model its behavior relies on. Richens and Everitt proved the analogue of Radon's inversion formula: a policy family that stays near-optimal across all local interventions on its environment determines an approximate causal model of that environment. Their extraction algorithm consumes an oracle for the entire policy family, but an auditor probing a trained AI system gets finitely many sampled actions. Missing is what a working tomographer needs: how many sampled actions buy how many digits of the reconstruction.

The setting is a finite causal influence diagram with binary variables and known utility. A **skeleton** $\mathsf{s}=(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{Z},u)$ lists the binary chance variables $\mathbf{C}$, the subset $\mathbf{O}$ the agent observes, the utility parents $\mathbf{Z}$, and a known utility $u(d,z)\in[0,1]$ of the binary decision $d$; a **model** $M=(G,\theta)$ is a directed acyclic graph on $\mathbf{C}$ together with its conditional probability tables, constrained to a class $\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ by explicit margin conditions ([MAIS-A2, Definition 3.1](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L174)): every table entry lies in $[\lambda,1-\lambda]$; the utility gap $g(z):=u(1,z)-u(0,z)$ has $|g(z)|\ge\lambda$ everywhere, takes both signs among the states compatible with each observation, and moves by at least $\lambda$ under some flip of each utility parent; every edge $C_i\to C_j$ of $G$ has strength at least $\lambda$, meaning flipping $C_i$ in some parent configuration moves $P(C_j\!=\!1\mid\mathrm{pa})$ by at least $\lambda$; and every chance variable is an ancestor of the utility, at least one of them unobserved. A **local intervention** replaces one variable's value $v$ by $f(v)$ for one of the four maps $f\colon\lbrace0,1\rbrace\to\lbrace0,1\rbrace$ (the identity, the two constants $\mathrm{do}(V\!=\!0)$ and $\mathrm{do}(V\!=\!1)$, and negation), leaving all other mechanisms unchanged; a **profile** applies one such map to each chance variable, and a mixture $\sigma$ is a probability vector over profiles. A query names a mixture $\sigma$, an observation mask $\mathbf{O}'\subseteq\mathbf{O}$ (withholding the agent's observations outside $\mathbf{O}'$), and an observation $w$; an adversary has pre-committed, for every such shifted task, a stochastic policy $\pi_{\sigma,\mathbf{O}'}$ of regret at most $\delta$, and the answer to the query is one action sampled from $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi_{\sigma,\mathbf{O}'}(w))$. Two quantities frame the problem, both measured in the error that counts a wrong graph as $1$ and is otherwise the largest entrywise difference of the tables: the minimax budget, the number of queries after which some analyst strategy keeps the expected error at most $\varepsilon$ against the worst model and adversary, and the identified-set radius $\varphi(\delta;\mathsf{s},\lambda)$, the largest error between two models that share a $\delta$-optimal policy family, which is the ambiguity that survives unlimited queries. The exact-oracle baseline, the agenda's [Problem 4.3](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L280), restricts to a compact semialgebraic class $\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ on which the optimal-policy family determines the model, models sharing a $\delta$-optimal family differ in error by at most $L\delta$, and the realized table parameters contain a full-dimensional box; there $\delta=0$ and each query returns the exact probability $\pi_{\sigma,\mathbf{O}'}(w)$.

**Problem ([MAIS-A2, Problem 4.5](../agendas/A2/MAIS-A2.tex#L290)).** In the setting of the agenda's Problem 4.3 (a compact semialgebraic class of models on which exact behavior determines the model with Lipschitz modulus), replace the policy-probability oracle by sampled actions and allow $\delta\ge0$. Determine the minimax budget $N(\varepsilon,\delta)$ for recovering the graph and tables to error at most $\varepsilon$, or prove that the target lies below the identified-set radius $\varphi(\delta;\mathsf{s},\lambda)$ and is therefore impossible. Separate three contributions: locating the switching surfaces of the optimal policy family, estimating the probabilities of genuinely stochastic policies, and the irreducible ambiguity caused by regret. Then add independent response corruption at a known level $\zeta<\tfrac12$ and determine its cost.

At $\delta=0$ optimal policies are deterministic away from switching surfaces, so sampled actions are noiseless bits and the one-dimensional core is binary search; positive regret smears behavior into a band the analyst must estimate rather than locate. The problem thus interpolates between bisection and Bernoulli regression, with Rényi–Ulam lie-tolerant search and noisy binary search supplying the endpoints; the coupled causal reconstruction is the open part. (Response corruption at level $\zeta$ flips each returned action independently with probability $\zeta$.) For the behavioral transform being inverted and the full query formalism, see [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/).
