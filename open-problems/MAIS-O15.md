# Explicit thresholds in parametric bounded Löb

*Open problem MAIS-O15 · posed in [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/) as [Problem 4.6](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L382) · Status: open.*

*Tags: cooperative AI · agent foundations · Löbian cooperation · proof-based agents · bounded rationality · logic · complexity theory. Difficulty: ★★.*

*Authored by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine · Audited by: GPT 5.6 Sol.*

The parametric bounded Löb theorem is an existence theorem: given a proof of the reflection scheme $\forall k\ (\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k) \to p(k))$, it produces *some* threshold $\hat{k}$ above which $p(k)$ is provable. An agent allocating a proof-search budget needs the threshold itself. How does $\hat{k}$ depend on the data?

Fix an efficient proof system $S$: following Critch, a consistent, recursively axiomatized extension of Peano arithmetic with binary numerals (the numeral $\overline{k}$ has $O(\log k)$ symbols) and an abbreviation rule, with all proof lengths counted in symbols. The default $\mathsf{PA}_{\mathrm{bin}}$ is Peano arithmetic in a Hilbert-style calculus with these features, fixed in [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/) down to the Gödel coding. Here $\mathrm{Lang}_1(S)$ denotes the formulas of $S$ with at most one free variable, $|p|$ the length of the formula $p$ in symbols, and $\ell_S(\varphi)$ the least symbol length of an $S$-proof of $\varphi$; for $p \in \mathrm{Lang}_1(S)$, $\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k)$ asserts that the instance $p(\overline{k})$ has an $S$-proof of at most $f(k)$ symbols. $\mathcal{B}$-terms are the search-free budget terms generated by constants, $k$, and the numeral-length function $\lambda(k) = |\overline{k}| + 1$ under addition, multiplication, composition, and $x \mapsto 2^x$, with $|f|_{\mathcal{B}}$ the length of the term $f$.

Three hypotheses enter the statement. The expansion function $\mathcal{E} \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ prices the act of noticing a proof, as a function of the budget alone, uniformly over formulas: every $\varphi$ with an $S$-proof of at most $m$ symbols has an $S$-proof of $\Box_m\ \varphi$ of at most $\mathcal{E}(m)$ symbols, and $S$ proves $\Box_m\ \varphi \to \Box_{\mathcal{E}(m)}\ \Box_m\ \varphi$. The *standing expansion certificate* is an $S$-proof that a $\mathcal{B}$-term $E^\sharp$ bounds it: $S \vdash \forall m\ (\mathcal{E}(m) \le E^\sharp(m))$. A budget $f$ is *internally regular* if its graph is provably total and single-valued and, for every standard integer $b \ge 1$, the system $S$ proves $\mathcal{E}(2\lambda(k)) + b\,\lambda(k) \le f(k)$ for all $k$ beyond some standard threshold: $S$ itself certifies that $f$ eventually covers the cost of noticing a proof at numeral-length scale plus any fixed multiple of the numeral length. Finally, the comparison conjecture, Conjecture 4.1 ([MAIS-O13](MAIS-O13.md)), asserts that such certificates come for free inside the budget algebra: whenever one $\mathcal{B}$-term is at most another for all sufficiently large standard $k$, the system $S$ proves the inequality beyond some threshold, with the threshold and the proof computable from the two terms. The repaired theorem's proof is effective once its internal budget comparisons are supplied, which is what the conjecture provides.

**Problem ([MAIS-A1, Problem 4.6](../agendas/A1/MAIS-A1.tex#L382)).** Fix $S$ with the standing expansion certificate, and assume Conjecture 4.1 ([MAIS-O13](MAIS-O13.md)). Given $p \in \mathrm{Lang}_1(S)$, an internally regular $\mathcal{B}$-term $f$, and an $S$-proof of $\forall k\ (\Box_{f(k)}\ p(k) \to p(k))$ of length $L$, give explicit bounds. Writing $C_S$ for the fixed constants of the proof system, require

$$\hat{k} \ \le\  K\bigl(L, |p|, |f|_{\mathcal{B}}, C_S\bigr), \qquad \ell_S\bigl(\forall k > \hat{k}\ p(k)\bigr) \ \le\  \Lambda\bigl(L, |p|, |f|_{\mathcal{B}}, C_S\bigr),$$

and determine how small $K$ and $\Lambda$ can be made. In particular, for $f(k) = k$ and a certified linear expansion bound, can both be polynomial in $L + |p|$?

The first bound caps the threshold; the second caps the length of the proof that certifies everything beyond it. A polynomial answer for $f(k) = k$ would make the FairBot cooperation threshold effectively computable rather than merely finite. For the repaired theorem and the certificate formalism in full, see [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/); for the refutation and repair, [MAIS-P2](../papers/P2/).
