# Geometry of fibers of the multiplication map of deep linear neural networks

*Summary [LR24] · S. P. Lehalleur and R. Rimányi, 2024 · [arXiv:2411.19920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19920).*

*Tags: generalization · singular learning theory · developmental interpretability · algebraic geometry.*

*Summarized by: Claude Fable 5 directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** For a deep linear network, the weight tuples whose product is a fixed matrix $B$ form an algebraic variety. The paper determines this fiber's codimension and number of top-dimensional irreducible components in three equivalent forms. Comparing the codimension formula with Aoyagi's earlier computation shows that the real log canonical threshold of squared error equals half the codimension, the largest value allowed by the general bound.

## Setting

A deep linear network with dimension vector $\underline d = (d_0, \dots, d_N)$ is the multiplication map $\mathrm{mult}\colon (A_1,\dots,A_N) \mapsto A_N \cdots A_1$ from tuples of composable matrices to $d_N \times d_0$ matrices. The object of study is the fiber $\mathrm{mult}^{-1}(B)$ over a fixed matrix $B$: the exact-fit locus of the network, hence the zero set of the squared-error loss $K_B(A) = \lVert \mathrm{mult}(A) - B \rVert^2$. These fibers are the model case for the level-set geometry that singular learning theory cares about — the optimal set on which the learning coefficient stratifies the parameters. The tool is quiver representation theory: the fibers are invariant under the natural change-of-basis group action, and their geometry is organized by the orbit structure of type-$A$ quiver representations.

## Main results

1. **Codimension and component count.** The codimension $C$ of $\mathrm{mult}^{-1}(B)$ and the number $\theta$ of its irreducible components of maximal dimension are determined, in any depth $N$, in three equivalent forms: as the lowest-degree term $\theta q^C$ of a Poincaré series in equivariant cohomology; as the optimal value and number of optimizers of a quadratic integer program; and by an explicit formula suited to asymptotics. Neither $C$ nor $\theta$ changes when the entries of the dimension vector are permuted.
2. **General threshold properties used by the paper (Proposition 8.4).** The authors recall that the local real log canonical threshold is lower semicontinuous and that $\mathrm{rlct}(F)\le \tfrac12\operatorname{codim}F^{-1}(0)$, referring to prior work for these general facts.
3. **Comparison with Aoyagi (Theorem 8.6).** Aoyagi had computed the threshold of the deep-linear loss. Comparing his formula with the paper's codimension formula gives
   $$\mathrm{rlct}(K_B)=\tfrac12\operatorname{codim}\mathrm{mult}^{-1}(B).$$
   The fiber can be singular, but its global threshold is not reduced below this codimension benchmark.

## Method

The change-of-basis group acts on tuples of matrices with finitely many orbits, indexed by rank data from type-$A$ quiver representations. The fiber decomposes along this orbit structure; equivariant cohomology packages the dimensions of the pieces into a Poincaré series, whose lowest term yields the codimension and component count and can also be expressed as a quadratic integer program. The threshold statement follows by comparing the resulting codimension formula with Aoyagi's theorem.

## Why it matters for AI safety

Singular learning theory uses the geometry of an exact-fit set to predict Bayesian complexity, while developmental interpretability seeks quantities that distinguish mechanisms within that set. This paper makes the global geometry of a deep-linear fiber computable: its codimension and top-dimensional components are explicit. It does not compute the local learning coefficient and multiplicity at every weight tuple; that finer stratification is the remaining problem in [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/). The lower-semicontinuity fact used there is general background recalled by this paper, not a new theorem of the authors.
