# Geometry of fibers of the multiplication map of deep linear neural networks

*Summary [LR24] · S. P. Lehalleur and R. Rimányi, 2024 · [arXiv:2411.19920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19920).*

*Tags: generalization · singular learning theory · developmental interpretability · algebraic geometry.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** For a deep linear network, the set of weight tuples whose product is a fixed matrix $B$ is an algebraic variety; the paper determines its codimension and the number of its irreducible components of maximal dimension, in three equivalent forms, and shows that the real log canonical threshold of the squared-error loss equals half that codimension — the largest value the threshold can take. In Watanabe's terms, deep linear networks are only *mildly* singular. Along the way the authors prove that the local real log canonical threshold is lower semicontinuous in the point, a general fact about real-analytic functions.

## Setting

A deep linear network with dimension vector $\underline d = (d_0, \dots, d_N)$ is the multiplication map $\mathrm{mult}\colon (A_1,\dots,A_N) \mapsto A_N \cdots A_1$ from tuples of composable matrices to $d_N \times d_0$ matrices. The object of study is the fiber $\mathrm{mult}^{-1}(B)$ over a fixed matrix $B$: the exact-fit locus of the network, hence the zero set of the squared-error loss $K_B(A) = \lVert \mathrm{mult}(A) - B \rVert^2$. These fibers are the model case for the level-set geometry that singular learning theory cares about — the optimal set on which the learning coefficient stratifies the parameters. The tool is quiver representation theory: the fibers are invariant under the natural change-of-basis group action, and their geometry is organized by the orbit structure of type-$A$ quiver representations.

## Main results

1. **Codimension and component count.** The codimension $C$ of $\mathrm{mult}^{-1}(B)$ and the number $\theta$ of its irreducible components of maximal dimension are determined, in any depth $N$, in three equivalent forms: as the lowest-degree term $\theta q^C$ of a Poincaré series in equivariant cohomology; as the optimal value and number of optimizers of a quadratic integer program; and by an explicit formula suited to asymptotics. Neither $C$ nor $\theta$ changes when the entries of the dimension vector are permuted.
2. **Lower semicontinuity (Proposition 8.4(ii)).** For a real-analytic function $F$ on a real-analytic manifold, the local real log canonical threshold $x \mapsto \mathrm{rlct}_x(F)$ is lower semicontinuous, so the sublevel sets $\{x : \mathrm{rlct}_x(F) \le c\}$ are closed in the zero locus. In general one also has the inequality $\mathrm{rlct}(F) \le \tfrac12 \operatorname{codim} F^{-1}(0)$.
3. **Mild singularity (Theorem 8.7).** For the deep-linear loss $K_B$ the inequality is an equality: the real log canonical threshold equals half the codimension of the fiber. The smooth-model value is attained, which is what "mildly singular" means — the singularities of the exact-fit locus do not depress the learning coefficient below its regular benchmark.

## Method

The change-of-basis group acts on tuples of matrices with finitely many orbits, indexed by rank data as in the representation theory of type-$A$ quivers. The fiber decomposes along this orbit structure; equivariant cohomology bookkeeps the dimensions of the pieces, packaging codimension and component count into a Poincaré series whose extraction is the quadratic integer program. The threshold computation then rides on the component geometry of the fiber.

## Why it matters for AI safety

Singular learning theory predicts that Bayesian learning concentrates on the flattest strata of the optimal set — those of smallest learning coefficient — and the program of [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/) builds effective dynamics on those strata. That program needs two things this paper supplies. First, the $\lambda$-stratification must be an honest geometric object: lower semicontinuity of the threshold closes the sublevel sets, the part of the frontier condition of [MAIS-O72](../open-problems/MAIS-O72.md) that is now known. Second, the stratification must be computable in a concrete model: the component and codimension data for the deep-linear fiber is the partial progress recorded in [MAIS-O77](../open-problems/MAIS-O77.md), which asks for the full local invariants $(\lambda, m)$ at every point of the fiber. For the surrounding program, see [MAIS-A7](../agendas/A7/).
