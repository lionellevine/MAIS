# The lengths of proofs

*Summary [P98] · P. Pudlák, in Handbook of Proof Theory (S. R. Buss, ed.), Elsevier, 1998, pp. 547–637.*

*Tags: logic · complexity theory · proof-based agents · Löbian cooperation · bounded rationality.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** A survey of proof length as a complexity measure: how long the shortest proofs of given theorems are, how the answer depends on the proof system, and what the incompleteness phenomena look like when proofs are counted by symbols. Three of its threads carry the quantitative Löb program of MAIS-A1: the derivability conditions of provability logic hold with tracked polynomial length bounds; the finite consistency statement $\mathrm{Con}_S(n)$ is provable in $S$ in length polynomial in $n$ but not in length below $n^{\varepsilon}$; and dependence on coding conventions is tamed by working up to polynomial simulation.

## Setting

Fix a first-order theory $S$ (Peano arithmetic is the running example) in a concrete calculus, and measure a proof by its number of symbols; $S \vdash_{\le n} \varphi$ means $\varphi$ has an $S$-proof of at most $n$ symbols. Bounded provability is itself arithmetized: $\Box_n\varphi$ is the formalized statement that such a proof exists, and $\mathrm{Con}_S(n) := \neg\Box_n\bot$ says that no contradiction has a proof of at most $n$ symbols — a finite, decidable fragment of the consistency of $S$. To compare calculi, one proof system *polynomially simulates* another if proofs translate between them with at most polynomial blowup; results proved up to polynomial simulation are independent of the coding conventions within the simulation class.

## Main results

The chapter is ninety pages; these are the results the agenda leans on.

1. **Length-tracked derivability conditions.** The Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions, and formalized $\Sigma_1$-completeness, hold with explicit polynomial length bounds: from an $m$-symbol proof of $\varphi$ one can construct a proof of the arithmetized statement "$\varphi$ is provable" of length polynomial in $m$, and likewise one step up ($\Box\varphi \to \Box\Box\varphi$ with polynomial-length proofs). This is the internalization step of every Löbian argument, now with a price tag.
2. **Finite consistency statements.** For a large class of theories including $\mathsf{PA}$ (results of Friedman and Pudlák, presented in the chapter): there is $c$ such that $S \vdash_{\le n^c} \mathrm{Con}_S(n)$, and there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that every $S$-proof of $\mathrm{Con}_S(n)$ has more than $n^{\varepsilon}$ symbols. The bounded shadow of Gödel's second theorem thus lies between two polynomials, with the exponent gap still open.
3. **Speedup.** Passing to a stronger theory can shorten proofs drastically — Gödel's speedup phenomenon, which the chapter develops quantitatively along with the self-referential machinery behind it.

## Method

Arithmetization with the bookkeeping kept honest: efficient numerals and syntax codings so that formalized syntax has polynomial-size codes, and proof translations tracked step by step. The lower bounds run through Parikh's self-referential sentence "I have no proof of at most $n$ symbols" — the finite-domain cousin of the Gödel sentence — combined with the upper bounds themselves: a too-short proof of the finite consistency statement would yield a short proof of a contradiction.

## Why it matters for AI safety

An agent that trusts another only after verifying a proof about its behavior pays in proof length, so the feasibility of proof-based cooperation is a question about how long proofs must be — a resource classical provability theory ignores and this chapter systematically counts. It is the standard toolbox for that accounting, and [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/), which prices the bounded Löb argument in symbols, draws on it throughout. The length-tracked derivability conditions are exactly what is needed to bound the agenda's two system constants — the expansion function $\mathcal{E}_S$ and the diagonalization cost $d_S$ ([MAIS-O12](../open-problems/MAIS-O12.md)) — and thereby to make the polynomial-overhead ledger explicit ([MAIS-O10](../open-problems/MAIS-O10.md)). The Friedman–Pudlák bounds on $\mathrm{Con}_S(n)$ are the nearest proved relatives of the agenda's target overhead function: quantitative bounds for the one corner of Löb's setup ($P = \bot$) where the hypothesis is unprovable. And polynomial simulation is the classical remedy proposed for the convention-dependence worry of [MAIS-O21](../open-problems/MAIS-O21.md). For the ledger these tools feed, see [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/).
