# The implicit bias of gradient descent on nonseparable data

*Summary [JT19] · Z. Ji and M. Telgarsky, COLT 2019 · [arXiv:1803.07300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07300).*

*Tags: generalization · training dynamics · optimization · dynamical systems.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** Gradient descent on the logistic loss, run on an arbitrary bounded dataset, produces iterates that follow a unique ray determined by the data. The direction of the ray is the maximum-margin predictor of a maximal linearly separable subset of the examples, approached at rate $O(\ln\ln t/\ln t)$; the ray's offset is the bounded risk minimizer over the remaining examples, recovered at rate $O((\ln t)^2/\sqrt t)$. Where Soudry et al. identify the limiting direction on separable data, this paper supplies the rates and the finite part of the iterate, with no separability assumption.

## Setting

Logistic regression: a linear predictor $w\mapsto w\cdot x$ trained by gradient descent on the average logistic loss over a finite dataset, with a small constant step size. No separability or non-degeneracy is assumed — the data may be separable, nonseparable, or a mixture. The analysis rests on a decomposition of the dataset into two parts: a maximal subset that can be separated with positive margin, and a complementary part confined to a subspace on which the risk behaves strongly convexly and attains a finite minimum. On fully separable data the second part is empty and the results specialize to a refined form of the max-margin theorem of Soudry et al. [[SHNGS18]](SHNGS18.md).

## Main results

1. **Ray convergence.** The iterates are biased to follow a unique ray: an unbounded component growing logarithmically along the maximum-margin direction of the separable part, plus a bounded offset equal to the risk minimizer over the nonseparable part. The risk itself converges to its infimum, which is not attained when the separable part is nonempty.
2. **Directional rate.** The direction of the iterate converges to the maximum-margin direction of the separable part at rate $O(\ln\ln t/\ln t)$.
3. **Offset rate.** The component of the iterate transverse to the separable span converges to the finite optimum at rate $O((\ln t)^2/\sqrt t)$.

The point for a user of the theorem is the second-order information: not just where the direction ends up, but how fast, and what finite piece of the iterate survives after the logarithmically divergent part is subtracted.

## Method

The separable/nonseparable decomposition is defined by convex duality and is unique. The iterate is then tracked in two orthogonal pieces. On the complement of the separable span the risk is strongly convex, and standard descent bounds give convergence to the finite optimum. Along the separable span the loss can only decay by escaping to infinity; a smoothed-margin argument shows the escape happens along the maximum-margin direction of the separable part, at logarithmic speed, and the interplay between the two pieces yields the explicit rates.

## Why it matters for AI safety

In the agenda's coin-line model of goal misgeneralization, the max-margin endpoint is uninformative twice over: it is the same intended-goal direction for every diversity level $\varepsilon>0$, and it discards the bounded part of the iterate entirely. Both of the coin line's open questions live in exactly the information this paper adds. The crossover time of [MAIS-O84](../open-problems/MAIS-O84.md) — how many steps the rare corrective examples take to overturn the proxy — is a finite-time question that needs the rates; the boundary-state residual of [MAIS-O89](../open-problems/MAIS-O89.md) — the part of the trained classifier not explained by the max-margin direction, which controls half the test return — is a question about the subtracted finite piece. The refined residual and rate analysis here is the tool both problems name; the max-margin computations they refine are in [MAIS-A8](../agendas/A8/).
