# The implicit bias of gradient descent on nonseparable data

*Summary [JT19] · Z. Ji and M. Telgarsky, COLT 2019 · [arXiv:1803.07300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07300).*

*Tags: generalization · training dynamics · optimization · dynamical systems.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** Gradient descent on logistic or exponential loss follows a data-determined ray even when the dataset is not linearly separable. The ray points in the maximum-margin direction of a canonical separable part of the data and is offset by the finite risk minimizer on the complementary part. For the general mixed case, the stated parameter rates use steps $\eta_t=1/\sqrt{t+1}$; the fully separable directional result also has a unit-step version.

## Setting

The paper studies linear prediction under logistic or exponential loss, with bounded examples and gradient descent initialized at $w_0=0$. No separability or non-degeneracy is assumed: the data may be separable, nonseparable, or mixed. A canonical decomposition splits the data into a part whose projection is linearly separable and a complementary subspace on which the risk attains a finite minimum and has enough curvature for parameter convergence. The main general-data parameter bounds use the decaying schedule $\eta_t=1/\sqrt{t+1}$; some risk bounds allow any $\eta_t\le1$, and the fully separable directional theorem is also stated for $\eta_t=1$.

## Main results

1. **Ray convergence.** With the theorem's step schedules, the iterates follow a unique ray: an unbounded component along the maximum-margin direction of the separable part, plus a bounded offset equal to the risk minimizer on the complementary subspace.
2. **Directional rate.** Under $\eta_t=1/\sqrt{t+1}$ for general data, the normalized iterate approaches the ray direction at rate $O(\ln\ln t/\ln t)$. A corresponding bound holds in the fully separable case with unit steps.
3. **Offset rate.** Under the same decaying schedule, the projection onto the finite-risk subspace approaches its optimum at rate $O((\ln t)^2/\sqrt t)$.

The point for a user of the theorem is the second-order information: not just where the direction ends up, but how fast, and what finite piece of the iterate survives after the logarithmically divergent part is subtracted.

## Method

The separable/nonseparable decomposition is defined by convex duality and is unique. The iterate is then tracked in two orthogonal pieces. On the complement of the separable span the risk is strongly convex, and standard descent bounds give convergence to the finite optimum. Along the separable span the loss can only decay by escaping to infinity; a smoothed-margin argument shows the escape happens along the maximum-margin direction of the separable part, at logarithmic speed, and the interplay between the two pieces yields the explicit rates.

## Why it matters for AI safety

Goal misgeneralization asks which of several training-compatible rules gradient descent selects, and when that selection becomes visible. The maximum-margin direction alone can miss both finite-time behavior and a bounded component that still affects predictions. This paper gives a rigorous decomposition and rates for linear models, making it a useful template for [MAIS-A8](../agendas/A8/). Applying its numerical rates requires matching the paper's loss, data decomposition, initialization, and step schedule.
