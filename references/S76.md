# Provability interpretations of modal logic

*Summary [S76] · R. M. Solovay, Israel Journal of Mathematics 25 (1976), 287–304.*

*Tags: Löbian cooperation · proof-based agents · logic · agent foundations.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** Read the modal box as "provable in Peano arithmetic." Which propositional laws does this reading obey? Solovay's answer is exact: Gödel–Löb logic $\mathsf{GL}$ — the base modal logic $K$ plus the Löb axiom $\Box(\Box A \to A) \to \Box A$ — proves a modal formula if and only if every arithmetical realization of it is a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$. A second theorem characterizes the laws that are *true* (in the standard model) rather than provable. The completeness direction embeds an arbitrary finite Kripke countermodel into arithmetic by a self-referential construction, so that a single realization refutes each non-law.

## Setting

A **realization** assigns to each propositional atom a sentence of arithmetic; connectives commute, and $\Box \psi$ becomes the arithmetized statement "$\psi$ is provable in $\mathsf{PA}$," via a standard provability predicate. A modal formula is a **law of provability** if every realization of it is a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$. The soundness direction was already understood: the distribution axiom and necessitation are the Hilbert–Bernays derivability conditions, the Löb axiom is the internalized form of Löb's theorem. The open question was whether $\mathsf{GL}$ proves *all* the laws.

## Main results

1. **Arithmetical completeness.** For every modal formula $\varphi$: $\mathsf{GL} \vdash \varphi$ if and only if every arithmetical realization of $\varphi$ is a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$. In particular, each non-law is refuted by a single explicit realization.
2. **The logic of true principles.** The modal formulas all of whose realizations are *true* in the standard model are exactly those provable in the system obtained from the theorems of $\mathsf{GL}$ by adding the reflection schema $\Box A \to A$ and closing under modus ponens only (necessitation fails: $\Box A \to A$ is true but not a law of provability).

## Proof method

For the hard direction, take a formula not provable in $\mathsf{GL}$; by the (previously known) Kripke semantics for $\mathsf{GL}$ it fails at the root of some finite, transitive, irreflexive Kripke model. Solovay embeds this model into arithmetic: using the diagonal lemma he defines a recursive function $h$ that climbs the model's nodes, moving to a node exactly when it finds a proof that the limit of $h$ is *not* that node, and realizes each atom by the disjunction of the sentences "$h$ has limit $i$" over the nodes $i$ forcing the atom. Provable facts about $h$ mirror the accessibility relation closely enough that the model's refutation transfers: the realization of the formula is not a theorem of $\mathsf{PA}$. The construction is uniform and survives soundness assumptions weaker than truth of $\mathsf{PA}$; the second theorem is proved by an extension of the same embedding.

## Why it matters for AI safety

Proof-based agents — programs that cooperate when they find a proof, within a length budget, that their counterpart cooperates — reason about each other by the laws of provability, and Solovay's theorem is the exact inventory of those laws in the unbounded case. It is therefore the target theorem for the bounded provability logic sought in [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/): the soundness half becomes the conjecture that every $\mathsf{GL}$-law survives when each "provable" is read as "provable within $k^c$ symbols" ([MAIS-O19](../open-problems/MAIS-O19.md)), and the completeness half becomes the question of whether Solovay's self-referential construction survives length accounting ([MAIS-O22](../open-problems/MAIS-O22.md)) — a question that, per the agenda, resists even precise formulation. Everything in [S76] is qualitative, with no proof lengths anywhere; the agenda's program is to find out how much of it becomes quantitative. See [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/).
