# A parametric, resource-bounded generalization of Löb's theorem, and a robust cooperation criterion for open-source game theory

*Summary [C19] · A. Critch, J. Symbolic Logic 84 (2019) 1368–1381 · [arXiv:1602.04184](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04184).*

*Tags: cooperative AI · open-source game theory · Löbian cooperation · proof-based agents · bounded rationality · logic.*

*Summarized by: Claude 5 Fable directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** Löb's theorem describes idealized provers with unlimited resources; this paper proves a parametric, proof-length-bounded version and applies it to computable agents that read each other's source code. The agent $\mathrm{FairBot}_k$ — cooperate if you find a proof, within a length budget, that the opponent cooperates back; otherwise defect — cooperates with its own copies once $k$ is large enough, and more generally any two "$G$-fair" bounded agents mutually cooperate in the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma once both proof budgets exceed a finite threshold. The cooperating programs are computable and unexploitable, and the criterion is robust: unlike the program equilibria of Tennenholtz and Fortnow, it needs no test for program equality, so agents with entirely different implementations can still recognize each other as cooperators.

## Setup and hypotheses

Fix a first-order proof system $S$ extending Peano Arithmetic that represents computable functions, writes the numeral $k$ in $O(\log k)$ symbols, and admits abbreviations in proofs. Proof length is measured in characters: write $\vdash_n \phi$ if $\phi$ has an $S$-proof of at most $n$ characters, and let the **bounded box** $\Box_k\phi$ be the arithmetic sentence "$\phi$ is provable in at most $k$ characters" (extended, via an evaluation convention, to formulas with a free parameter). A **proof-expansion function** $\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ is fixed large enough that the system can notice its own bounded proofs: if $\vdash_k \phi$ then $\vdash_{\mathcal{E}k} \Box_k\phi$ (bounded necessitation), and $\vdash \Box_k\phi \to \Box_{\mathcal{E}k}\Box_k\phi$ (bounded inner necessitation). Two supporting lemmas with explicit character costs, implication distribution and quantifier distribution, handle the modal bookkeeping. Asymptotic comparisons use $f \prec g$, meaning $g$ eventually dominates every constant multiple of $f$. The author argues that $\mathcal{E}(k) \in O(k)$ is reasonable to expect for proof systems engineered for easy checking, but no bound is computed for a specific system.

## Main results

1. **Parametric diagonal lemma (Prop. 1).** For any formula $G$ with an extra Gödel-number argument there is a formula $\psi$ with $\vdash \forall \bar k\; \psi(\bar k) \leftrightarrow G(\ulcorner\psi\urcorner, \bar k)$ — a single diagonal fixed point holding uniformly in the parameters $\bar k$.
2. **Parametric bounded Löb (Thm 3).** Let $p$ be a formula with one parameter and $f$ a computable function with $f(k) \succ \mathcal{E}\,O(\log k)$. If $\vdash \forall k,\; \Box_{f(k)}\, p(k) \to p(k)$, then there is a threshold $\hat k$ with $\vdash \forall k > \hat k,\; p(k)$. Informally: if the system proves, uniformly in $k$, that a proof of $p(k)$ within budget $f(k)$ would make $p(k)$ true, then $p(k)$ is provable outright for all large $k$.
3. **Self-cooperation of $\mathrm{FairBot}_k$.** Taking $p(k)$ to be "$\mathrm{FairBot}_k(\mathrm{FairBot}_k) = \mathit{Cooperate}$," the theorem yields cooperation for all sufficiently large $k$ — against the intuitive infinite-regress argument ("each waits for the other's proof, so both defect") that the author reports mathematicians almost universally give at first.
4. **Robust cooperation of bounded agents (Thm 4).** Call $A_k$ **$G$-fair** if it provably cooperates whenever there is a proof, within budget $k + G(\text{length of the opponent's source})$, that the opponent cooperates with it. Under growth hypotheses on $G$ and the expansion function (roughly, $\mathcal{E}\,O(\log k) \prec k$ and $G$ increasing fast enough), any two $G$-fair agents $A_m, B_n$ mutually cooperate for all $m, n$ above a single threshold, without either testing the other for program equality.

## Proof method

A character-counting, parametric analogue of the classical diagonal-lemma-plus-Löb argument. The fixed point comes from Prop. 1; the proof then interpolates carefully chosen intermediate budget functions $g, h$ with $\mathcal{E}g \prec h \prec f$ and threads bounded necessitation, bounded inner necessitation, and the two distribution lemmas so that every step of the internalized argument stays below the budget $f(k)$. Theorem 4 is the instance of Theorem 3 at the predicate "all pairs of budgets beyond $k$ yield mutual cooperation."

## Why it matters for AI safety

AI agents can, in principle, exhibit their source code to one another — a form of transparency unavailable to humans, who cannot make their reasoning inspectable even when they want to. That asymmetry opens cooperation avenues closed to us, and this paper shows the simplest one works: one-shot interactions between open-source agents need not end at defect–defect, because bounded proof search about each other's behavior supports mutual cooperation that is stable, unexploitable, and indifferent to how the opponent is implemented. The same mechanism cuts both ways — agents that can verify cooperation with each other can in principle sustain agreements no human party can join or audit, collusion against their principals included — which is one reason the quantitative thresholds matter. Earlier modal-agent results established this for idealized agents with unlimited proof resources; this paper brings the phenomenon down to genuinely runnable programs. What it leaves qualitative is everything a deployment would need: the admissible budgets, the threshold $\hat k$, and the expansion function $\mathcal{E}$ are asymptotic, with no constants for any concrete proof system. Quantifying them is the subject of [MAIS-A1](../agendas/A1/), which also records that, under its representation conventions, the cooperation argument needs internal proofs of its budget comparisons, and states a repaired form of the theorem with that hypothesis made explicit.
