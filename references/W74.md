# Lower bounds on the maximum cross correlation of signals

*Summary [W74] · L. R. Welch, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 20(3), 397–399, 1974.*

*Tags: interpretability · superposition · convex geometry · optimization.*

*Summarized by: Claude Fable 5 directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** More than $n$ unit vectors in an $n$-dimensional space cannot all be nearly orthogonal: among any $m>n$ of them, some pair has squared inner product at least $(m-n)/(n(m-1))$. This three-page classic, written for the design of low-interference signal sets in multichannel communication, is now the basic packing obstruction throughout frame theory and compressed sensing — and, in the superposition literature, the reason a network storing $m$ features in $n<m$ dimensions cannot make all pairwise interference small.

## Setting

A signal set is a collection of $m$ unit-norm vectors $u_1,\dots,u_m$ in an $n$-dimensional inner-product space, and its quality for multichannel communication is measured by the worst cross-correlation $c_{\max}=\max_{i\ne j}|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|$: distinct users' signals should interfere as little as possible. When $m\le n$ one can take an orthonormal set and achieve $c_{\max}=0$; the question is how small $c_{\max}$ can be when the number of signals exceeds the dimension.

## Main results

The bound now called the **Welch bound**: any $m>n$ unit vectors satisfy

$$c_{\max}^2 \;\ge\; \frac{m-n}{n(m-1)}.$$

In words, once the dimension is exhausted, the worst pairwise correlation is bounded away from zero by an explicit function of how overloaded the space is; as $m\to\infty$ the right side tends to $1/n$, so $c_{\max}\gtrsim 1/\sqrt n$ no matter how the vectors are arranged. Two remarks on how the bound is used in this repo. First, the proof actually bounds the *average* squared cross-correlation, so the same lower bound holds for the root mean square, not just the maximum. Second, rearranged as a cardinality bound (this form is [MAIS-A4](../agendas/A4/)'s, not Welch's): if every pairwise correlation is at most $c$ with $c<1/\sqrt n$, then $m\le n(1-c^2)/(1-nc^2)$ — a hard cap on how many directions a coherence budget admits. Welch's paper also proves a family of higher-order bounds, one for each even power $2k$ of the correlations, obtained from the same argument applied to tensor powers; only the $k=1$ case is used here. Equality in the $k=1$ bound is attained precisely by equiangular tight frames, which exist only for special pairs $(m,n)$ — this characterization is later literature, not part of [W74].

## Proof method

Two lines of linear algebra. The Gram matrix $G=(\langle u_i,u_j\rangle)$ is positive semidefinite with trace $m$ and rank at most $n$, so Cauchy–Schwarz on its at most $n$ nonzero eigenvalues gives $\|G\|_F^2\ge m^2/n$. Subtracting the $m$ diagonal ones, the $m(m-1)$ off-diagonal entries have squared sum at least $m(m-n)/n$, and the largest is at least the average. ([MAIS-A4](../agendas/A4/) reproduces this proof as part of its Lemma 4.1.) The higher-order bounds replace $G$ by its entrywise $k$-th power, whose rank is at most $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$.

## Why it matters for AI safety

In the ReLU toy model of superposition, column $W_i$ of the weight matrix is the direction where feature $i$ is stored, and the coherence $\mu(W)$ — the largest normalized inner product between distinct columns — is the worst-case crosstalk between two stored features. The Welch bound is the reason legibility has a price: $m>n$ stored features force $\mu$ above an explicit threshold, so a coherence cap $c<1/\sqrt n$ forces the network to drop features outright, at least $m-n(1-c^2)/(1-nc^2)$ of them, each costing a computable amount of loss. That is one of the two proved anchors of the interpretability–performance frontier, and it also exposes a trap in the natural training fix: the smooth average-interference penalty drives the dictionary toward a tight frame, but tight frames obey the Welch bound like everything else, so the penalty cannot push worst-case coherence to zero by adjusting angles — only by shrinking columns. The frontier between these anchors is the subject of [MAIS-A4](../agendas/A4/).
