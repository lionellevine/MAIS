# Composing global solutions to reasoning tasks via algebraic objects in neural nets

*Summary [T25] · Y. Tian, NeurIPS 2025 · [arXiv:2410.01779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01779).*

*Tags: interpretability · mechanistic interpretability · universality of circuits · representation theory · harmonic analysis.*

*Summarized by: Claude Fable 5 directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** For two-layer networks with quadratic activation and squared loss trained on abelian group multiplication, the space of global (zero-loss) solutions across widths carries a semi-ring structure: partial solutions compose, via the ring operations, into global ones. The framework (CoGS) constructs rich families of inequivalent exact optimizers analytically — many mechanisms, not one — and in experiments about 95% of gradient-descent solutions match the constructed forms, with weight decay biasing toward the simpler constructions over memorization.

## Setting

The task is multiplication in a finite abelian group (modular addition being the model case), read from one-hot inputs; the network is two-layer with quadratic activation, trained on the $\ell^2$ loss over the full multiplication table. The object of study is not a single trained network but the set of exact solutions across all widths, viewed as an algebraic structure.

## Main results

1. **Algebraic structure.** Weight space, taken across widths, carries a semi-ring structure, and the loss decomposes into *sum potentials* that behave as ring homomorphisms with respect to the ring operations.
2. **Composition.** Because the potentials are homomorphisms, partial solutions — weights that zero out some of the potentials — combine under the ring operations into global solutions that zero them all. This turns the construction of exact optimizers into algebra.
3. **Abundance.** The composition mechanism yields explicit families of inequivalent global optimizers, well beyond the single Fourier construction: for this activation and loss, the zero-loss set contains many genuinely different circuits.
4. **Empirics.** Roughly 95% of solutions found by gradient descent match the constructed forms. Overparameterization helps asymptotically, and weight decay favors the simpler constructed solutions over memorization-type ones.

## Method

Purely algebraic where the theory goes: define ring operations on weights (across widths), verify that each summand of the loss is a homomorphism for them, and then generate global solutions by composing per-component partial solutions — for abelian groups the natural components are the Fourier frequencies (group characters). The claim that trained networks land near the constructed families is empirical, established by matching gradient-descent solutions against the catalog.

## Why it matters for AI safety

Universality — the hope that independently trained networks converge on the same internal mechanism — has a static prerequisite and a dynamical one. This paper settles the static half in the negative for its regime: inequivalent zero-loss circuits exist in abundance, so nothing about the loss landscape alone forces a unique mechanism. What remains open is the dynamical half — whether gradient flow from random initialization actually charges more than one of these mechanisms with positive probability — which is the subject of the non-identifiability problems in [MAIS-A5](../agendas/A5/), and nothing analogous is yet proved for cross-entropy loss or nonabelian groups such as $S_5$. Separately, the constructed families are candidate witnesses in the minimal-width question of [MAIS-A6](../agendas/A6/): if any of them descend to the architecture there, they could lower the known upper bound.
