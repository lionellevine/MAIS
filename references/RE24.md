# Robust agents learn causal world models

*Summary [RE24] · J. Richens and T. Everitt, ICLR 2024 (oral) · [arXiv:2402.10877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877).*

*Tags: interpretability · world-model discovery · eliciting latent knowledge · causal inference · probability.*

*Summarized by: Claude Fable 5 directed by Lionel Levine.*

**TL;DR.** In a single-decision causal model, an agent that satisfies a regret bound across a specified family of interventions has a policy containing enough information to reconstruct an approximate causal model. The approximation improves as regret decreases. The reconstruction is constructive but assumes access to the agent's policy in every shifted domain, together with the known utility and intervention family.

## Setup and hypotheses

The environment is a causally sufficient causal Bayesian network $M=(G,P)$ over chance variables $\mathbf C$; the decision task is a single-decision, single-utility causal influence diagram with known real-valued utility $U$, and the agent sets a policy $\pi(d\mid \mathrm{pa}_D)$ to maximize expected utility. Regret is $\delta:=\mathbb E^{\pi^*}[U]-\mathbb E^{\pi}[U]$. Two structural assumptions: the decision affects only the utility (*unmediated task*), and strategically relevant latent variables exist (*domain dependence*). Distributional shifts are *local interventions* — maps applied to the states of a single variable, chosen because they require no prior knowledge of $G$ — together with all mixtures of them. A $\delta$-optimal policy oracle returns a regret-$\le\delta$ policy in each shifted domain.

## Main results

1. **Necessity, optimal case (Theorem 1).** For almost all such diagrams (the exceptions are a Lebesgue-null set of fine-tuned parameters), the DAG $G$ and the joint distribution restricted to the ancestors of $U$ are identifiable from the optimal policies over all mixtures of local interventions.
2. **Necessity, bounded regret (Theorem 2).** From regret-$\delta$ policies one can identify an approximate model whose conditional probability tables are within $\gamma(\delta)$ of the truth, with $\gamma(0)=0$ and $\gamma$ linear for small $\delta$; for $\delta>0$ only a subgraph of sufficiently strong edges is guaranteed.
3. **Sufficiency (Theorem 3).** Given the causal model one can compute optimal policies for any utility and any soft intervention; an $\epsilon$-approximate model yields policies whose regret grows linearly in $\epsilon$.
4. **Equivalence.** Together: learning an approximate causal world model is necessary and sufficient for regret-bounded transfer under local interventions.

## Proof method

Constructive identification via the policy oracle. For almost all parameters each shift has a unique optimal decision; querying the oracle along a one-parameter mixture between a shift and a hard intervention locates a critical mixing weight (an indifference point), and since the utility is known, that weight pins down one interventional quantity. Iterating outward from the utility parents through the ancestors of $U$ recovers every table and the latent DAG. With regret $\delta$ the critical weight is only bracketed, which propagates to the linear error bound. The algorithm is validated empirically on random binary-latent diagrams.

## Why it matters for AI safety

The result makes robust behavior informative about an agent's environment model: under its assumptions, the family of shifted policies contains enough information to recover causal structure and probabilities. This is an information-theoretic statement about the policy, not proof that the agent stores an explicit causal graph in a human-readable internal representation. Turning policy access into a finite-sample auditing procedure with explicit margins and error bounds is the subject of [MAIS-A2](../agendas/A2/).
